## wilderness

A clear progression could be:

Hearth → Bounds → Borderlands → Deepwild → Untamed

1. **Hearth** = inside the village; protected, inhabited, familiar
   - “hearthland” suggests the safe, familiar territory around one’s home or village.
2. **Bounds** = fields, roads, and woods within the village’s safe reach
   - **Bounds** are still clearly within the village’s sphere of control.
     They include nearby fields, roads, pastures, managed woods, watch posts, and places people use regularly.
     Danger may exist, but the community can usually respond to it.
   - **Bounds** = still inside the safe perimeter
3. **Borderlands** = beyond regular protection, but still travelled or patrolled
   - **Borderlands** are the transition zone where that control starts to weaken.
     They may still be mapped, patrolled, or travelled, but not reliably.
     Threats from the wild are more common, and people who go there are expected to be more self-sufficient.
   - **Borderlands** = at the edge where safety becomes uncertain
   - the shift from Bounds to Borderlands is essentially the moment when “the village protects you” becomes “you are mostly responsible for yourself.”
4. **Deepwild** = remote wilderness where human influence fades
   - **Deepwild** is wilderness that is far from settlement and difficult to reach, but still somewhat known.
     Hunters, rangers, traders, or explorers may occasionally pass through it.
     There might be old trails, ruins, landmarks, or maps.
     It is dangerous mainly because civilization is distant and help is unavailable.
   - **Deepwild** = beyond civilization’s reach
   - **Deepwild** says “people rarely come here”
5. **Untamed** = extremely wild, unknown, and dangerous territory
   - **Untamed** goes further: it is land that humanity has never truly controlled, settled, or even fully understood.
     Trails may disappear entirely. Maps become unreliable.
     The landscape, creatures, weather, or supernatural forces dominate completely.
     Humans are visitors there, not masters of it.
   - **Untamed** = beyond civilization’s influence
   - **Untamed** says “this place does not belong to people at all”





For a more formal or map-like system:

Settlement → Hinterland → Frontier → Wilderness → Primeval Wilds

For a darker fantasy tone:

Hearthland → Wardlands → Marches → Wilds → Outer Wild

“The Marches” is especially useful for the semi-safe territory between civilisation and true wilderness.


## concepts

### Hearth

is the floor or area directly in front of a fireplace.

Traditionally, the hearth was the warm center of a home, where people cooked, gathered, and kept warm. Because of this, the word also symbolises:

home
family
safety
comfort
settled civilisation

Example: “Far from the warmth of the hearth, the forest became dangerous.”


### Hinterland

The land behind or beyond a town, coast, or settled area. It is usually rural, less populated, and connected economically or geographically to the settlement.
Example: “The village traded with farms in the surrounding hinterland.”

### Primeval

Extremely ancient, especially from a time before human civilisation. It often suggests something untouched, original, and powerful.
Example: “A primeval forest” means an ancient forest that feels unchanged since earliest times.

### Hearthland

A poetic or fantasy-style word built from hearth, the fireplace and symbolic centre of a home. It suggests safe, inhabited land around homes and settlements. It is not a standard geographic term.
Implied meaning: protected, familiar country where people live.

### Wardlands

Another fantasy-style term. Ward means protection, guarding, or a magical defence. Wardlands would be territory protected by guards, walls, patrols, laws, or magical wards.
Implied meaning: land still under the settlement’s protection, even outside its centre.

### Marches

Border territories between settled regions, kingdoms, or civilisation and wilderness. Historically, marches were frontier zones governed and defended because they were exposed to danger or invasion.
Example: “The northern marches” means the kingdom’s remote northern borderlands.


In terms of safety, they might suggest:

Hearthland — fully safe and settled
Wardlands — protected outer territory
Marches — risky frontier
Hinterland — remote surrounding country
Primeval wilds — ancient, untouched wilderness
